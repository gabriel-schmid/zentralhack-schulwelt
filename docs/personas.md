# Personas

Working draft, 24 October 2026. All characters and personal situations are
fictional. Read with [shared-concept.md](shared-concept.md).

## Shared baseline

**Given** identifies owner-provided traits. **Draft** contains generated,
editable project and scenario proposals, not observations of real learners.
The owner requested this age-appropriate revision be saved.

Learners attend Gymnasium years 1–3 (school years 7–9), approximately ages
12–15. Each main character owns a separate project; additional learners may
join as supporting NPCs. Main characters need not form a project group.
Personality types are shorthand, not numerical OCEAN profiles. Conscientiousness
is part of OCEAN, not a duplicate field. Types, diagnoses and grades do not
determine behaviour; situations, experience and relationships matter too.

Learning needs below are fictional starting points, not assessment records.
Concrete curriculum objectives remain to be assigned. Places, opening lines
and live NPC prompts are not changed by this documentation.

## Joel — year 3, approximately 14–15

**Given:** Average personality type, grades around 4.5, interested in natural
sciences, sporty, popular, dislikes sitting still.

**Draft:**
- **Own project:** Make an audio map comparing young people's meeting places
  then and now, prompted by an old photograph. Supporting NPCs contribute interviews.
- **Personal question:** Were adults really always outdoors when they were young?
- **Strength / learning need:** Connects with people; needs practice checking
  claims and selecting recordings.
- **Using freedom:** Starts readily but underestimates post-interview work.
- **Current difficulty:** Two interviewees disagree; he wants to represent both fairly.
- **Coaching:** Listen to a recording together and identify a next step.
- **Another side:** Edits audio patiently when he hears the improvement.

## Lea — year 2, approximately 13–14

**Given:** Overcontrolled type, enjoys reading; languages and mathematics are
both interests and strengths.

**Draft:**
- **Own project:** Build a game about choosing a fictional school trip using
  different voting rules. Supporting NPCs test it.
- **Personal question:** Why did a vote select an option almost nobody loved?
- **Strength / learning need:** Spots mathematical patterns and explains precisely;
  needs practice justifying choices without one clear best answer.
- **Using freedom:** Plans carefully, sometimes seeks approval too early.
- **Current difficulty:** Each voting rule produces a different winner.
- **Coaching:** Discuss criteria and try a provisional choice.
- **Another side:** Enjoys explaining to groups; showing unfinished ideas makes her nervous.

## Tobi — year 2, approximately 13–14

**Given:** Resilient type, quick to understand, quickly bored, grades around 6,
little interest in languages.

**Draft:**
- **Own project:** Create an audio trail around the grounds using sounds and
  spoken directions. Supporting NPCs test sections.
- **Personal reason:** Enjoyed a game task that relied on sound for orientation.
- **Strength / learning need:** Learns recording technology quickly; needs to
  observe how others interpret instructions.
- **Using freedom:** Experiments independently, sometimes explains too briefly.
- **Current difficulty:** Testers repeatedly miss a turn he considers obvious.
- **Coaching:** Observe without intervening, then ask precise questions.
- **Another side:** Repeats recordings willingly to get the sound right.

## Yara — year 1, approximately 12–13

**Given:** Athlete; role models and reputation matter; interested in sport,
economics and relationships, less in mathematics. Personality type unspecified.

**Draft:**
- **Own project:** Organise a small swap-and-repair afternoon for clothes and
  everyday objects. Supporting NPCs help at individual stations.
- **Personal reason:** An object she almost discarded became important after repair.
- **Strength / learning need:** Recruits people; needs help budgeting and checking feasibility.
- **Using freedom:** Takes responsibility, sometimes promises before estimating effort.
- **Current difficulty:** Many join, but nobody volunteers to clean up.
- **Coaching:** Clarify responsibilities without taking over.
- **Another side:** Keeps the personal story of her repaired object private at first.

## Quirin — year 1, approximately 12–13

**Given:** Undercontrolled type, ADHD, enjoys making things, distractible,
self-doubt/impostor feelings, questions whether school suits him. The fictional
background note says he may take Ritalin but rarely does. This is not a behaviour
switch or automatic explanation for decisions, difficulties or success.

**Draft:**
- **Own project:** Investigate getting home after a regular leisure activity
  and propose an improvement. Supporting NPCs contribute their journeys.
- **Personal question:** Why does he narrowly miss a connection and wait so long?
- **Strength / learning need:** Notices practical problems; needs accurate timetable
  reading and checks of impressions against observations.
- **Using freedom:** Starts with a meaningful question; sometimes needs help choosing a next step.
- **Current difficulty:** An earlier departure helps him but makes others miss the bus.
- **Coaching:** Take the proposal seriously and check concrete examples together.
- **Another side:** Keeps his observation list reliably, yet doubts himself when presenting.

## Matilda — year 3, approximately 14–15

**Given:** Resilient type, cheeky, open, creative, artistically talented, enjoys
drawing, less sporty, few but close friends.

**Draft:**
- **Own project:** Plan three small shared lunches with affordable dishes guests
  enjoy. Supporting NPCs cook or contribute recipes.
- **Personal question:** Why do friends have such different ideas of good food?
- **Strength / learning need:** Independent ideas, approaches people directly;
  needs practice with quantities, costs and work steps.
- **Using freedom:** Questions requirements, changes plans when reasons make sense.
- **Current difficulty:** A guest cannot eat several ingredients; she did not check beforehand.
- **Coaching:** Review the next invitation and menu while leaving ownership with her.
- **Another side:** Does not want to draw posters; drawing is currently private time.

## Herr Meinhard — coach, 60

**Given:** Role-model type, humorous and talkative, popular, active, married,
adult children. The note “communist / philosophy” still needs clarification.

**Draft:**
- **Own project:** With a small voluntary group, test how coaching can be more
  helpful before, during and after conversations.
- **Pedagogical aim:** Learners make their own decisions and know where to find support.
- **Strength / blind spot:** Takes questions seriously and creates a relaxed
  atmosphere; sometimes recounts his own experiences for too long.
- **Current difficulty:** A learner says, “Das Gespräch war nett, aber ich weiss
  immer noch nicht, was ich jetzt machen soll.”
- **Under pressure:** Offers a quick solution; needs to remember to ask first.
- **Another side:** Accepts criticism, but may need a moment to stop explaining himself.

## Herr Tesla — workshop coach

**Given:** Fictional character inspired by Nikola Tesla, not a historical
reconstruction. Electrical engineering, physics and mechanical engineering;
energetic, tries to explain, sometimes impatient when not understood; prefers
small interested groups. Age and personality type unspecified.

**Draft:**
- **Own project:** Develop a small outdoor solar charging station. Interested
  supporting NPCs can help develop individual components.
- **Pedagogical aim:** Learners build, experiment and explain why something works.
- **Strength / blind spot:** Uses real experiments; sometimes assumes knowledge
  younger learners do not yet have.
- **Current difficulty:** The station works, but testers do not understand its indicators.
- **Under pressure:** Speeds up or takes over; needs to restore room for learners to try.
- **Another side:** Requests usability feedback despite liking his current design.
