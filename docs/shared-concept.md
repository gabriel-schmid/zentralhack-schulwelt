# Shared simulation concept

Status: working baseline confirmed in the concept discussion on 24 October 2026.
This describes the intended model, not completed implementation or evidence of effectiveness.

## Purpose

Make a different school day tangible through a simulation with learner and
teacher/coach NPCs, built from a shared persona template. Visitors should be
able to examine whether school provides enough room for intrinsic motivation,
personal interests, meaningful choice, specialisation and practical relevance.
The simulation should make both possibilities and tensions visible.

## Confirmed decisions

- The framework curriculum and the learning objectives derived by teachers or
  the school define binding minimum requirements. Teachers retain some scope
  in deriving those objectives.
- Minimum objectives are assessed. Failure to meet them can lead to failing a
  subject or stage under the applicable regulations.
- The existing grading regulations remain in force. A specific regulation,
  school type and curriculum have not yet been selected for the simulation.
- Freedom applies from the beginning, including the route towards minimum
  requirements. It is not a reward unlocked after passing an assessment.
- Learners have broad freedom over their learning within the binding school
  framework. The exact scope of individual choices still needs definition.
- Coaches advise and support learners according to the school's pedagogical
  principles and guidelines. Learning itself cannot be forced.
- Formative assessment supports learning and indicates whether progress towards
  minimum objectives is sufficient. It should make the risk of failing the
  summative assessment and subject visible early.
- Disciplinary measures remain possible as a last resort under school rules
  when no other way has been found in cases of refusal. Their exact triggers
  and relationship to learning decisions remain unresolved.

## Design priorities

- Leave room for intrinsic motivation; do not require every learner to feel
  interested in every activity.
- Allow transfer of shared competencies to learners' particular interests.
- Make the purpose of learning understandable to learners. The question
  «Wieso lerne ich das?» remains legitimate.
- Internal differentiation means differences between individual personas;
  external differentiation means differences between groups (the terms used
  in this concept discussion).
- Show opportunities for specialisation alongside shared minimum objectives.
- Define time structures, spaces, curriculum/objectives, formative assessment
  and summative assessment explicitly.

## Assumptions to examine

The concept owner expects formative assessments to give sufficiently clear
indications of progress and risk. This is a design assumption to test in the
simulation, not an established finding. Include uncertain evidence, individual
contributions hidden by group products and learners who need more support.

The simulation can illustrate consequences of its rules and assumptions.
It cannot establish that this school model improves real motivation or learning.
Claims about current schools are questions motivating the project, not findings
established by the simulation.

## Open decisions

1. School type, age range, framework curriculum, concrete minimum objectives
   and applicable grading/promotion regulations.
2. Actual choices concerning topics, methods, pace, groups, products, places
   and assessment dates/forms; distinguish proposals from confirmed freedoms.
3. Time structures, space/material limits, staffing and coaching capacity.
4. Formative evidence, feedback frequency, learner access to evidence and
   handling of unclear or contradictory indications.
5. Responses when progress stalls: support, discussion, agreements and the
   limits of coach authority. Keep difficulty, disagreement over a task and
   refusal distinguishable. The disciplinary boundary requires explicit rules.
6. Individual summative evidence when learning happens through group projects;
   a project presentation alone is not yet an agreed assessment model.
7. Common persona fields, changing states, knowledge boundaries and how
   decisions have persistent consequences in the simulation.
8. First scenario and intended visitor experience. The existing raised-bed
   project is a candidate, not a confirmed selection.

## Useful situations to explore (proposals)

- An engaged learner pursues a project that leaves a minimum objective uncovered.
- A learner cannot yet name an interest or prefers clear guidance.
- A strong group product conceals uneven individual understanding.
- Several learners need the same coach at the same time.
- A learner understands the risk of failing but declines the recommended route.

## Mathematics demo decisions (24 October 2026)

The visitor observes a school day and forms their own opinion. The first slice
follows Lea (year 2) and Joel (year 3) in mathematics with different class goals.
Teacher videos and comprehension questions precede application. Preparation is
provided within BSZ; home preparation is optional. Both have completed it when
the scene starts. Shared checkpoints alternate with accompanied self-study.
Different support needs are task-specific scenario assumptions. See
[the demo documentation](mathematics-demo.md) for implementation and scope.
